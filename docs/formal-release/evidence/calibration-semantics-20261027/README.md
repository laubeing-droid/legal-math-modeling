The tower-property theorem says that a prediction equal almost everywhere to the conditional mean on the observation information remains calibrated on the smaller score information when the score is measurable there. In particular, score information generated by a measurable function of the observations is within that contract. The conditioning information and probability measure are explicit.

The square-loss theorem works for square-integrable response and prediction functions and a prediction measurable with respect to the conditioning information. It proves the actual conditional risk equals conditional variance plus the squared distance from the conditional mean. Its equality case characterizes the unique conditional optimum almost everywhere. For binary responses, a separate theorem proves the conditional variance is `q*(1-q)` with `q` the actual conditional mean, and another obtains the binary Brier decomposition. These statements do not assume the desired risk decomposition, and they do not assert that a finite training algorithm has found that optimum or that deployment preserves the law.
