It records that the first marginals of `sourceInput ⊗ₘ labels` and `targetInput ⊗ₘ labels` are their respective input measures. Given `targetInput ≪ sourceInput`, the induced joint measures are absolutely continuous, and the joint RN derivative agrees almost everywhere with the input RN derivative evaluated at `x`. For measurable loss in `[0,1]`, the proof derives target-joint integrability, weighted source-joint integrability, and the equality of the target risk integral with the source-joint integral weighted by the true input RN derivative. `estimated_weight_error_identity` additionally requires the estimated weighted loss itself to be integrable and leaves its signed error integral explicit; it does not assume zero error or provide an estimation rate. The result gives integrability, not a bounded weighted loss or concentration guarantee. A bounded original loss alone does not bound an unbounded density ratio.
- **L121 — randomized unseen-input boundary.** `randomized_unseen_input_lower_bound` reuses `u01_3_two_worlds_same_training_uncontrolled_deployment`. It makes the two randomization weights nonnegative and summing to one, keeps the visible training objects identical, and proves that one of the two hidden-label worlds has expected point risk at least `1/2`. This is the stated binary randomized two-world construction; it does not claim a separate sharpness theorem or a universal OOD guarantee.
- **L123 — same input marginal, different label mechanism.** `same_input_marginal_different_label_mechanism` uses the distinct joint laws `Dirac(true, false)` and `Dirac(true, true)`. Their actual `X` marginals coincide, the conditioning atom has probability one in both, and the conditional label rates are respectively zero and one. Every detector that reads only `X` has the same pushforward output law in both worlds. The reused `finite_group_and_deployment_boundaries` supports the separate boundary that an exchangeability condition does not automatically survive a changed deployment law.
