# Legal Privilege Does Not Determine Differential-Privacy Epsilon

**Author:** Laupinco

## Abstract

This paper examines a specific impossibility boundary in `legal-math-modeling`: a legal privilege or release class does not, by itself, determine a unique differential-privacy parameter $\varepsilon$. Earlier project materials proposed or explored a direct privilege-to-epsilon mapping. The repository now records the corresponding `T18_DPPrivilege` claim as `REFUTED`, supported by counterexample-oriented Python and SMT artifacts rather than by a Lean theorem. The negative result must be read narrowly. It does not prove that differential privacy is impossible for legal data, that no policy may select epsilon, or that every legal classification is irrelevant to disclosure. It shows that a legal label alone underdetermines a quantitative privacy budget unless an external technical and institutional policy supplies adjacency, mechanism, sensitivity, composition horizon, utility objective, jurisdiction, data context, and approval provenance. We restate the differential-privacy definition, construct a two-policy witness to non-uniqueness, distinguish inconsistency from underdetermination, and integrate the result with the repository’s formal boundaries: probability-kernel edges require an observation declaration; assurance aggregation retains pending legal inputs; trust meet cannot upgrade either input; authority cannot be generated by model consensus; and empirical annotations cannot rewrite normative solutions. Current Lean results are labelled `FORMALIZED`; the non-uniqueness argument and repository counterexamples are labelled `DERIVED/COUNTEREXAMPLE`; proposed policy loaders and composition theorems are `CONJECTURE`. The paper turns a failed universal mapping into a useful design rule: epsilon must be explicit, scoped, versioned, approved, and independently verified rather than presented as a mathematical consequence of legal privilege.

**Keywords:** differential privacy; legal privilege; epsilon; underdetermination; counterexample; privacy policy; formal methods

## 1. Research question and scope

The central question is not “what epsilon should law choose?” It is whether a privilege label logically entails one epsilon. The answer in the current repository is no. That answer must be kept distinct from several neighboring claims: whether a specified mechanism is differentially private, whether a particular epsilon is acceptable under a policy, whether data release is lawful, and whether legal privilege maps to a release prohibition rather than a noise parameter.

The manuscript uses three labels. `FORMALIZED` denotes a current Lean definition or theorem. `DERIVED/COUNTEREXAMPLE` denotes a mathematical argument or executable counterexample not established as a Lean theorem. `CONJECTURE` denotes a proposed policy or proof absent from the repository. The label `REFUTED` is preserved for the repository’s broad claim that legal privilege determines epsilon.

## 2. Related work

Differential privacy bounds the change in output distributions when one individual’s record changes [@DworkEtAl2006; @DworkRoth2014]. Mechanism design may use sensitivity and noise calibration, while composition accounts for repeated use [@NissimEtAl2007; @AbadiEtAl2016]. These technical guarantees are conditional on a defined dataset adjacency relation, randomized mechanism, measurable events, and budget.

Legal privilege, confidentiality, sealing, professional secrecy, and access control are normative or institutional categories. They may constrain whether processing or disclosure is permitted, but they do not share one universal numerical scale. The present paper does not offer comparative doctrine. It addresses a logical modeling error: treating a legal category as if it mathematically implied a complete DP mechanism parameter.

## 3. Differential privacy requires more than epsilon

Let \(D\sim D'\) mean that datasets are adjacent under a declared relation. A randomized mechanism \(M\) is \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-differentially private when, for every measurable output event \(S\),

$$
\Pr[M(D)\in S]\le
e^\varepsilon\Pr[M(D')\in S]+\delta.
\tag{1}
$$

Equation (1) is a standard background definition [@DworkEtAl2006; @DworkRoth2014], not a theorem proved by the repository. Pure epsilon-DP sets \(\delta=0\):

$$
\Pr[M(D)\in S]\le
e^\varepsilon\Pr[M(D')\in S].
\tag{2}
$$

Specifying $\varepsilon$ alone does not define $M$, adjacency, or the protected unit. A DP claim is at least a tuple

$$
DPClaim=(\mathcal D,\sim,M,\varepsilon,\delta,scope,version).
\tag{3}
$$

Equation (3) is `CONJECTURE` as a repository carrier. It is a methodological decomposition. Two systems using the same epsilon may protect different records or disclose different information because their adjacency and mechanisms differ.

For a real-valued query \(f\), global sensitivity is

$$
\Delta f=\sup_{D\sim D'}|f(D)-f(D')|.
\tag{4}
$$

The Laplace mechanism commonly chooses scale

$$
b=\frac{\Delta f}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{5}
$$

Equations (4)–(5) are background formulas, not `FORMALIZED` repository results. They show why epsilon is inseparable from the query and adjacency. A legal label does not determine $\Delta f$.

## 4. The rejected direct mapping

Let \(P\) be a set of legal privilege or release classes. The rejected proposal has the form

$$
\varepsilon_{law}:P\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}.
\tag{6}
$$

To say that law determines epsilon uniquely would require

$$
\forall p\in P,\exists!e\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}:LegalEntails(p,e).
\tag{7}
$$

Equation (7) is the target being denied. The current Lean ULM package does not define `LegalEntails` or prove its negation. Repository metadata in `JC_Formalization.lean` marks `T18_DPPrivilege` as `REFUTED`, and strict-proof artifacts construct countermodels. The proper status is `DERIVED/COUNTEREXAMPLE`, not `FORMALIZED`.

The design error is visible even before selecting doctrine. A class \(p\) may constrain release modes but leaves mechanism variables open:

$$
\Theta=(adjacency,M,\Delta f,\delta,k,utility,risk,context).
\tag{8}
$$

If epsilon is chosen by a policy \(g\), the honest signature is

$$
\varepsilon=g(p,\Theta,authority,version),
\tag{9}
$$

not equation (6). Equation (9) is `CONJECTURE` as a future policy interface. It does not claim every listed variable is necessary in every mechanism; it makes the external choice visible.

## 5. Two-policy witness to non-uniqueness

Fix one label $p_0$. Suppose policy context $c_1$ approves $\varepsilon=1$, while another context $c_2$ approves $\varepsilon=2.5$:

$$
g(p_0,c_1)=1,\qquad g(p_0,c_2)=2.5.
\tag{10}
$$

Then

$$
p_0=p_0\quad\land\quad g(p_0,c_1)\neq g(p_0,c_2).
\tag{11}
$$

Equations (10)–(11) are `DERIVED/COUNTEREXAMPLE`, reflecting the repository’s two-model witness. The numbers are policy-model constructions, not empirical legal facts and not recommended budgets. They show non-uniqueness only if both contexts are admissible under the premises of the proposed mapping.

Formally, define the possible-epsilon set

$$
E(p)=\{e\ge0\mid\exists c,Approved(p,c,e)\}.
\tag{12}
$$

If \(1,2.5\in E(p_0)\), then

$$
|E(p_0)|\ge2,
\tag{13}
$$

which refutes uniqueness in (7). Equation (13) is `DERIVED`. It does not establish that \(E(p)\) is the entire real line, unbounded, or nonempty for every privilege class. Those stronger statements require stronger premises and must not be inferred from two witnesses.

## 6. Underdetermination is not inconsistency

Two different approved values need not mean the policies contradict themselves. Each can be internally consistent relative to a different mechanism, utility target, or threat model. The problem is projection:

$$
(p,c_1)\mapsto 1,\qquad(p,c_2)\mapsto2.5
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad p\mapsto e\text{ uniquely}.
\tag{14}
$$

Equation (14) is `DERIVED`. Forgetting \(c\) creates a relation rather than a function:

$$
R_P\subseteq P\times\mathbb R_{\ge0},\qquad
(p,e)\in R_P\Longleftrightarrow e\in E(p).
\tag{15}
$$

If \(E(p)\) contains several values, \(R_P\) is multi-valued at \(p\). A policy can recover a function only by supplying a selection rule:

$$
select:P\times Context\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}.
\tag{16}
$$

Equations (15)–(16) are `DERIVED/CONJECTURE`: the relation follows from the witness construction; the specific selector is future policy. The selector’s existence does not make its output a theorem of law.

## 7. Composition makes context unavoidable

Under basic sequential composition, mechanisms with pure-DP budgets $\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_k$ have the familiar upper bound

$$
\varepsilon_{total}\le\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_i.
\tag{17}
$$

Equation (17) is background differential-privacy theory [@DworkRoth2014], not a repository theorem. More refined composition may yield different bounds [@AbadiEtAl2016]. A privilege label does not determine \(k\), the accounting method, or whether mechanisms act on the same individuals.

Budget allocation requires a policy such as

$$
(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_k)
\in\arg\max_{\mathbf e}\ Utility(\mathbf e)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad Accountant(\mathbf e)\le B.
\tag{18}
$$

Equation (18) is `CONJECTURE`. It exposes technical and institutional choices. Neither utility nor total budget \(B\) follows from privilege alone.

## 8. Zero, infinity, and prohibited release

It is tempting to encode absolute secrecy as $\varepsilon=0$ and public data as $\varepsilon=\infty$. That translation is not automatic. Under pure DP, epsilon zero requires identical output distributions for adjacent datasets:

$$
\varepsilon=0\Rightarrow
\Pr[M(D)\in S]=\Pr[M(D')\in S]
\tag{19}
$$

when the inequality is required in both adjacency directions. This is a technical property, not equivalent to a legal prohibition on processing or disclosure.

A prohibited release may instead be modeled as

$$
ReleaseAllowed(p,context)=false,
\tag{20}
$$

with no randomized mechanism executed. Equation (20) is `CONJECTURE` as a policy interface. It is often conceptually safer than forcing every legal prohibition onto the epsilon axis. Likewise, “no privacy requirement” is not a finite DP guarantee; it may mean that no DP claim is asserted.

## 9. What the Lean normal form actually says

`ULM01NormalForm.lean` includes `probabilityKernel` as an edge kind. `ULM04Obligations.lean` assigns an observation-declaration obligation to probability-kernel and ranker edges. This is `FORMALIZED`:

$$
kind(e)=probabilityKernel
\Longrightarrow observationDeclaration\in O_{req}(e).
\tag{21}
$$

The exact `requiredObligations` set also always contains type safety. The existence of an edge tag does not define a Markov kernel, probability space, adjacency, DP inequality, or composition accountant. Any assertion that “differential privacy guarantees are established” is therefore unsupported.

The verifier boundary is

$$
Sound(goal,v)\land Sat(v,subject)
\Rightarrow goal(subject).
\tag{22}
$$

This is `FORMALIZED` by `ULM04.sat_sound`. A future DP verifier could instantiate `goal` with a mechanism-specific property, but would still need an independently proved sound verifier and exact subject evidence. The current theorem does not provide that instantiation.

## 10. Legal input and assurance remain explicit

The assurance envelope records legal input separately. Pending and assumed references are independent finite sets. Combination is

$$
Pending(L_1\sqcup L_2)=Pending(L_1)\cup Pending(L_2),
\tag{23}
$$

$$
Assumed(L_1\sqcup L_2)=Assumed(L_1)\cup Assumed(L_2).
\tag{24}
$$

These equations are `FORMALIZED` in `ULM14CoverageTrust.lean`. If the authority or policy source for epsilon is missing, aggregation cannot silently remove that deficit.

The five-coordinate trust vector obeys

$$
(\tau\wedge\sigma)_i=\min(\tau_i,\sigma_i),\qquad
\tau\wedge\sigma\preceq\tau,\sigma.
\tag{25}
$$

This is `FORMALIZED`. A technically verified mechanism cannot raise a weak legal-authority coordinate, and authoritative legal classification cannot prove a mechanism-level DP inequality.

## 11. Authority is not generated by consensus

The broader Lean core defines authority ranks and valid one-step promotion receipts:

$$
receiptValid(r)\Longleftrightarrow
rank(r.to)=rank(r.from)+1.
\tag{26}
$$

It proves

$$
consensusRank([l]^n)\le rank(l).
\tag{27}
$$

These are `FORMALIZED` in `ReceiptAuthority.lean`. Repeating the same model opinion that $\varepsilon=1$ does not turn the value into an admitted policy. This does not deny possible accuracy gains from ensembles. It denies authority laundering.

A human receipt records task, input digest, reviewer, action, and validity fields; its two predicates bind only task plus digest and check only the time window plus revocation:

$$
bind(r,t,d)\Longleftrightarrow r.task=t\land r.digest=d,
\tag{28}
$$

$$
valid(r,now)\Longleftrightarrow
r.issued\le now\le r.expiry\land\neg r.revoked.
\tag{29}
$$

These are `FORMALIZED` in `HumanResearchReceiptSpec.lean`. The structure records an action, reviewer, task, and input, while the predicates prove only the stated field equalities and time/revocation conditions. They do not independently prove that the recorded action occurred, authenticate the reviewer, or show that the selected epsilon is legally or technically correct.

## 12. Empirical utility cannot rewrite normative constraints

Suppose empirical evaluation supplies a utility score \(u\). The ULM empirical attachment guarantees

$$
Attach(S,u).normativeSolutions=S.
\tag{30}
$$

This is `FORMALIZED` in `ULM15IncrementalEmpiricalBanach.lean`. A utility experiment can inform authorized policy but cannot automatically change the normative solution set.

A common privacy-utility optimization is

$$
\varepsilon^*\in\arg\max_{\varepsilon\in E_{approved}}
U(\varepsilon)-\lambda Risk(\varepsilon).
\tag{31}
$$

Equation (31) is `CONJECTURE`. The approved set, utility, risk, and $\lambda$ are not defined in Lean. Even a well-calibrated empirical optimum remains conditional on the policy that authorizes the feasible set.

## 13. A fail-closed epsilon policy proposal

A future policy object could be

$$
Policy=(scope,adjacency,mechanism,\Delta f,
\varepsilon,\delta,accountant,authority,version).
\tag{32}
$$

Equation (32) is `CONJECTURE`. Validation should require each field and exact subject identity:

$$
PolicyWF(P)\Longrightarrow
P.scope\neq\varnothing\land P.authority\neq\varnothing
\land P.mechanism\neq\varnothing.
\tag{33}
$$

The specific predicate is `CONJECTURE`; nonempty strings alone would not be sufficient for substantive validity. A missing field should return a partial or failure outcome:

$$
load(P)=
\begin{cases}
Complete(P),&PolicyWF(P),\\
Partial(P,O),&O\neq\varnothing,\\
Failure(e),&\text{invalid subject or mechanism}.
\end{cases}
\tag{34}
$$

Equation (34) is `CONJECTURE`, modeled on the `FORMALIZED` ULM outcome algebra. Mapping over the result must preserve failure and open obligations.

## 14. What the impossibility result does not say

The result does not state

$$
\forall p,\ E(p)=\varnothing.
\tag{35}
$$

Nor does it state

$$
\nexists M,\varepsilon,\delta:\ M\text{ is }(\varepsilon,\delta)\text{-DP}.
\tag{36}
$$

Equations (35)–(36) are claims explicitly **not** made. Differential privacy remains mathematically available when a mechanism and policy are specified. The counterexample concerns uniqueness of projection from one legal label.

It also does not prove

$$
LegalPrivilege\perp PrivacyRisk.
\tag{37}
$$

Legal classification may be highly relevant to release permission, risk appetite, access control, or the approved range. Equation (37) is neither defined nor proved. The correct conclusion is underdetermination, not irrelevance.

## 15. Detailed two-model construction

The counterexample can be stated without claiming that either model describes an actual jurisdiction. Let the shared legal vocabulary contain one release class \(p_0\). Model \(M_1\) adds a technical policy context \(c_1\) with an approved mechanism and budget \(e_1=1\). Model \(M_2\) keeps the same legal label but substitutes context \(c_2\) and budget \(e_2=2.5\). Each model is internally satisfiable because no common premise requires equality of the two policy values.

Write the model theories as

$$
\mathcal M_1\models Class(x)=p_0\land Approved(c_1,1),
\tag{38}
$$

$$
\mathcal M_2\models Class(x)=p_0\land Approved(c_2,2.5).
\tag{39}
$$

Equations (38)–(39) are `DERIVED/COUNTEREXAMPLE`, following the repository’s executable witness. They are not Lean propositions. The values are arbitrary constructions chosen to be unequal; they are not measurements, legal findings, or recommended privacy budgets.

The direct-mapping claim would require invariance across admissible contexts:

$$
\forall c_1,c_2,e_1,e_2,
Approved(p,c_1,e_1)\land Approved(p,c_2,e_2)
\Rightarrow e_1=e_2.
\tag{40}
$$

The witnesses violate the consequent of (40). Therefore the legal label alone is not sufficient under the model premises. This is a logical underdetermination result. It is not a contradiction between $\mathcal M_1$ and $\mathcal M_2$, because contexts differ.

A critic could restore uniqueness by adding a policy axiom:

$$
UniquePolicy(p):\quad |E(p)|=1.
\tag{41}
$$

Equation (41) is `CONJECTURE`. Adding it would define uniqueness rather than derive it from privilege. The policy would need provenance, scope, version, and authority. The counterexample therefore shifts the burden: anyone asserting equation (6) must state the premise that removes contextual freedom.

The same reasoning applies when epsilon is a range. If policies yield intervals \(I_1\) and \(I_2\), a unique legally entailed range would require

$$
ApprovedRange(p,c_1,I_1)\land ApprovedRange(p,c_2,I_2)
\Rightarrow I_1=I_2.
\tag{42}
$$

Equation (42) is `CONJECTURE` and can fail even when intervals overlap. Replacing a point with a range does not eliminate the need for a policy.

## 16. Mechanism-specific proof obligations

A positive DP theorem must quantify over a mechanism, not merely a parameter. For a proposed implementation \(M_v\), the proof subject should bind

$$
Subject_{DP}=(digest(M_v),adjacency,outputSpace,
\varepsilon,\delta,randomness,scope).
\tag{43}
$$

Equation (43) is `CONJECTURE` as a certificate schema. Each field can change the proposition. A proof for record-add/remove adjacency cannot certify substitute-one adjacency. A proof for a count query cannot certify arbitrary text generation. A proof for one mechanism digest cannot certify a later build.

Sensitivity must be proved for the exact query:

$$
\forall D\sim D',\quad |f(D)-f(D')|\le\Delta f.
\tag{44}
$$

Equation (44) is background DP obligation [@DworkRoth2014], not a repository theorem. Noise calibration then depends on the proved bound, not a legal category. If preprocessing \(P\) changes adjacency, the actual query is \(f\circ P\), and its sensitivity must be re-evaluated.

For an approximate-DP mechanism, verification must establish

$$
\sup_{D\sim D'}\sup_S
\left(\Pr[M(D)\in S]-e^\varepsilon\Pr[M(D')\in S]\right)
\le\delta.
\tag{45}
$$

Equation (45) is an equivalent-style proof target under appropriate measurable assumptions; it is background, not `FORMALIZED` here. Sampling finitely many datasets and outputs cannot establish the universal suprema.

Composition introduces another subject. A release composed from mechanisms \(M_1,\ldots,M_k\) should bind the adaptive schedule, shared population, and accountant:

$$
Accountant((M_i,\varepsilon_i,\delta_i)_{i=1}^{k})
=(\varepsilon_{tot},\delta_{tot}).
\tag{46}
$$

Equation (46) is `CONJECTURE` for the repository. Different accountants and assumptions give different bounds. The legal label does not select one.

Implementation verification also requires a randomness boundary. A mathematical Laplace mechanism assumes a distribution; code may use finite precision, clipping, truncation, or a faulty generator. A proof about the ideal distribution does not automatically transfer. The implementation must either refine the ideal mechanism or receive a narrower engineering claim.

Finally, the output channel matters. Releasing a DP statistic, retaining raw records, logging seeds, or attaching unnoised metadata are different systems. A complete claim must cover the observable transcript:

$$
Transcript(D)=(M(D),logs(D),metadata(D),errors(D)).
\tag{47}
$$

Equation (47) is `CONJECTURE` as a system model. Proving only the first coordinate can leave a privacy leak elsewhere.

## 17. Separating technical proof from legal permission

The technical judgment is

$$
TechnicalDP(Subject_{DP})\in\{proved,failed,pending\}.
\tag{48}
$$

The legal judgment is

$$
LegalPermission(scope,purpose,source,authority)
\in\{allowed,prohibited,pending\}.
\tag{49}
$$

Equations (48)–(49) are `CONJECTURE` as a combined policy interface. Neither implies the other. A mechanism can satisfy DP while its processing lacks lawful authority. A lawful disclosure can use a non-DP control where no DP representation is claimed.

A release gate should require both, without merging their evidence:

$$
ReleaseAllowed\Longrightarrow
TechnicalDP=proved\land LegalPermission=allowed
\land ScopeMatch.
\tag{50}
$$

Equation (50) is `CONJECTURE`; it states a conservative design, not universal law. Some applications may prohibit release regardless of technical protection. The point is that the legal branch is explicit.

The ULM assurance structure supports this separation. Specification, implementation, run check, coverage, legal input, and scope are distinct fields. Combination rejects incompatible scopes and preserves weak statuses. `FORMALIZED`: open specification references and pending legal references survive union. The structure does not define a DP-specific release policy.

## 18. Evaluation design

Evaluation should have four tracks. First, a logical track reruns the two-model witness and any SMT constraints. Its acceptance criterion is that both policy contexts satisfy the declared premises while their epsilon values differ. The report must say `COUNTEREXAMPLE`, not “Lean proved.”

Second, a formal-mechanism track states equation (43), formalizes adjacency and probability, and proves equation (1) or (45). This work is absent from the ULM package. A future proof should record assumptions about measurability, randomness, arithmetic, and implementation refinement.

Third, an engineering track tests the exact runtime. It checks parameter loading, sensitivity bounds, noise distribution diagnostics, composition accounting, failure paths, version binding, and absence of raw-data leakage through logs. Statistical tests can detect defects but cannot prove universal DP on their own.

Fourth, a legal-policy track identifies sources, purpose, jurisdiction, data class, authority, and decision. It should answer whether DP is permitted, required, insufficient, or irrelevant. Model-generated citations are candidates until reviewed. A human receipt records an asserted review action and can satisfy the model's binding and current-validity predicates; it does not by itself prove that the action occurred or entail legal correctness.

For empirical utility, a report may show a curve

$$
U(e)=\mathbb E[utility(M_e(D))],
\tag{51}
$$

but equation (51) is `CONJECTURE` until target, population, estimator, and data are specified. The curve cannot choose an approved epsilon without the policy and authority stages.

Evaluation artifacts should be subject-bound. At minimum, record code digest, input schema, mechanism configuration, epsilon, delta, adjacency, accountant, seed policy, runtime identity, and legal-policy version. A green job without these contents is insufficient.

## 19. Failure recovery

Failures should map to distinct outcomes. A missing epsilon authority is a legal-input obligation. A failed sensitivity proof is a formal-specification deficit. A runtime that emits the wrong noise distribution is an implementation or run failure. A composition-accounting overflow is not repaired by selecting a more favorable utility result.

A fail-closed state machine may be proposed as

$$
State\in\{Draft,PolicyPending,ProofPending,
RuntimePending,Blocked,Releaseable\}.
\tag{52}
$$

Equation (52) is `CONJECTURE`. Transition to `Releaseable` should require exact subject matches. Any change to mechanism, adjacency, epsilon, delta, accountant, or legal scope produces a new subject.

If the two-model counterexample succeeds, recovery is not to delete it. The direct mapping must remain refuted. A policy layer can be added under a new claim: “given approved policy \(P\), the selected epsilon equals \(P.epsilon\).” That is a configuration-consistency claim, not legal entailment.

If a DP proof fails, the system may change the mechanism, relax utility, revise adjacency, or block release. It must not silently increase delta, narrow the tested dataset, or replace a universal theorem with sampled evidence while retaining the same claim label. The evidence ledger must record the change.

If runtime verification is incomplete, the ULM outcome discipline requires nonempty open obligations. The certificate must not report mechanism privacy as complete. If legal review is pending, technical success cannot upgrade the authority coordinate. These recovery rules are `DERIVED` from `FORMALIZED` failure and assurance structures.

## 20. Adversarial tests and neighboring claims

An epsilon policy needs adversarial tests against accidental re-derivation from the legal label. Remove the policy file while keeping the privilege class fixed; the loader should become partial or blocked, not reconstruct a default number. Change the jurisdiction, purpose, adjacency, or mechanism digest; the old receipt should no longer satisfy exact subject identity. Duplicate an identical model recommendation; authority should not increase.

Sensitivity mutations are equally important. Change substitute-one adjacency to add/remove adjacency, alter clipping bounds, or add a derived field to the released query. A valid suite should detect that the old sensitivity proof no longer binds. Change the number of composed releases or accountant configuration; the old total budget must be invalidated.

Failure-channel tests should force the randomness provider to fail, emit nonfinite values, truncate noise, or leak raw values into logs. The runtime should return failure and preserve it through mapping. A fallback to an unnoised result would violate the fail-closed architecture even if the legal class normally permits release.

The impossibility claim has several neighbors that must remain separate. First,

$$
p\not\models\varepsilon=e
\tag{53}
$$

means that privilege label \(p\) alone does not entail the selected value. It does not imply

$$
(p,Policy)\not\models\varepsilon=e.
\tag{54}
$$

An approved policy can determine a configured number. Equations (53)–(54) are `DERIVED` distinctions.

Second, policy determination does not imply privacy:

$$
Policy.epsilon=e\centernot\Rightarrow M\text{ is }(e,\delta)\text{-DP}.
\tag{55}
$$

Equation (55) is `DERIVED`: the DP theorem still requires mechanism proof. Third, technical privacy does not imply lawful release:

$$
M\text{ is }(e,\delta)\text{-DP}
\centernot\Rightarrow LegalPermission=allowed.
\tag{56}
$$

Equation (56) is a boundary claim, not a universal legal theorem; legal permission is an independent input. Fourth, lawful release does not imply that DP is the chosen control:

$$
LegalPermission=allowed\centernot\Rightarrow
\exists e,\delta:\text{release claims }(e,\delta)\text{-DP}.
\tag{57}
$$

These non-entailments organize evidence. They prevent a true statement in one column from certifying another.

## 21. Interpretation and communication

A release note should say “the system used policy-approved $\varepsilon=e$ under configuration $P$” rather than “the law requires epsilon $e$.” If a mechanism theorem exists, it should add “the specified subject satisfies $(e,\delta)$-DP under adjacency $A$.” If legal review permits the release, that decision should be cited separately with its scope and authority.

The phrase “privacy level” is especially dangerous because it can refer to legal confidentiality, technical DP loss, access control, or subjective risk. A report should retain distinct fields. Smaller epsilon conventionally means a stronger DP bound for a fixed mechanism and adjacency, but it does not create a universal order over legal protection when utility, delta, composition, data types, and release prohibitions differ.

The counterexample should also be communicated narrowly. It refutes unique determination from a label under the stated premises. It does not show that every chosen epsilon is arbitrary. A detailed policy can justify a selection through technical risk, utility, governance, and law. The justification remains policy evidence rather than logical entailment from privilege.

For scholarly reproducibility, the words `REFUTED`, `DERIVED/COUNTEREXAMPLE`, and `FORMALIZED` must not be collapsed. `REFUTED` names the old direct mapping. `DERIVED/COUNTEREXAMPLE` names the current two-model argument. `FORMALIZED` applies only to ULM infrastructure such as obligation retention, trust non-upgrade, verifier soundness conditional, and receipt binding. No Lean DP theorem is claimed.

This vocabulary also supports correction. A later Lean formalization of the two-model relation could upgrade the proof form without changing the negative scope. A verified mechanism could add a separate positive theorem without reviving the direct legal mapping. Evidence can therefore improve incrementally while the refuted claim remains preserved as a guard against regression.

The distinction must survive abstracts, tables, certificates, user interfaces, and downstream citations. A shortened summary that omits it recreates the original modeling error even when the full technical appendix remains accurate.

## 23. Validation boundary

The non-uniqueness argument is not a Lean theorem. It is preserved as repository metadata and executable counterexample evidence. The current ULM axiom audit does not promote it. A future formalization could define the relation \(E(p)\), state two admissible policy witnesses, and prove non-uniqueness, but the admissibility premises would still come from an external policy or legal authority.

The standard DP equations are cited mathematical background. No repository release certificate should be interpreted as certifying a mechanism-level privacy guarantee. A valid guarantee must bind exact code, adjacency, data transformation, randomness, sensitivity, accountant, parameters, and subject. Testing sampled outputs cannot prove a universal DP inequality by itself.

Legal validity is independent again. Even a formally proved DP mechanism may process data unlawfully or violate a prohibition on disclosure. Conversely, a lawful release policy may use access control rather than DP. The model preserves this distinction through separate legal-input and proof coordinates.

## 24. Limitations

The paper does not conduct a comparative legal analysis of privilege. The numeric witnesses are synthetic policy choices. It does not independently prove the SMT artifacts sound, formalize probability theory in Lean, verify a randomness source, or analyze privacy attacks. It does not claim that the possible-epsilon set is unbounded for every class.

The proposal lacks a formal policy schema and runtime loader. Composition, subsampling, group privacy, approximate DP, Rényi DP, and privacy-loss accounting are outside the current ULM package. No empirical utility dataset is used. No specific epsilon is recommended.

The result is therefore a modeling boundary, not a privacy certification. Its practical force is to prohibit an unsupported derivation from legal label to number.

## 25. Reproducibility protocol

To reproduce the negative result, identify the exact privilege label and the premises of the claimed mapping. Exhibit two admissible contexts using the same label and different epsilon values, recording that the values are policy constructions. Verify that admissibility does not itself contain a hidden uniqueness rule. The witnesses refute uniqueness but not existence.

To reproduce a positive DP claim, specify equation (3) completely, prove the mechanism inequality under the declared adjacency, and bind the proof or verifier to exact code and parameters. Record composition scope and accountant. Then separately supply the legal authority permitting the processing and release.

Any missing element must remain pending or failed. A release certificate for unrelated Lean modules, a high empirical score, or repeated model agreement cannot fill the gap. This protocol makes both the negative and any future positive claim falsifiable.

## 26. Conclusion

Legal privilege does not uniquely determine epsilon. The repository correctly preserves this as a refuted direct-mapping claim supported by counterexample evidence, not as a Lean theorem about all law. Differential privacy remains available as a technical guarantee after adjacency, mechanism, sensitivity, accounting, parameters, scope, and authority are explicitly supplied. The ULM formal core contributes fail-closed infrastructure: observation obligations, exact subject verification, non-upgrading trust, retained legal-input deficits, record-level receipt constraints, and read-only empirical annotations. These structures turn impossibility into a design rule—epsilon is an externally authorized policy input, not a number inferred from a legal label.
